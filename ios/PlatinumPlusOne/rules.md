# The Constitution of the Platinum +1 Competition

## Preamble

We, the Judge and the Contestants of the Platinum +1 Club, in order to form a more perfect lounge, establish fair play, secure domestic tranquility in the cabin, provide for the common dining, promote the general welfare of the dark chocolate supply, and secure the blessings of the Platinum Dining Area to one guest and the Judge, do ordain and establish this Constitution.

## Article I. Name, purpose, and origin

Section 1. This body is the Platinum +1 Competition. Its standings page calls it the Platinum +1 Club.

Section 2. The Qantas International Lounge in Auckland holds the Platinum Dining Area, an invitation-only room with an à la carte menu from the chef Neil Perry. American Airlines Executive Platinum and Platinum Pro members may enter it, and each may bring one guest. That guest is the +1. This competition decides who the +1 is.

Section 3. One member of the party holds Executive Platinum status: Paul Bryar. The rest of the party holds Business Class tickets and the access that comes with them.

Section 4. Every member of the party, flying American Business Class, enters the Flagship Lounges at O'Hare and Dallas/Fort Worth on the way out and the main Qantas International Lounge in Auckland on the way back, without contest. Nothing in this Constitution affects that access.

Section 5. The competition descends from the Banff contest. On the trip home from Calgary, the Judge's Aspire Lounge access admitted two guests. He considered a fine of $5 a lapse, with the pot buying "#4" in at $32, and rejected it as punitive. He chose a point system that recognizes past accomplishments, and to a lesser extent shortcomings, and rewards future achievements.

Section 6. Points reset after Banff. No contestant carries a Banff total into this competition. The Banff scorecards are in Schedule A as precedent.

## Article II. The Judge

Section 1. Paul Bryar is the Judge. He enters every ruling from his phone.

Section 2. A ruling names one contestant and one value: +2, +1, -1, or -2. The Judge may attach a reason, and every reason is published.

Section 3. The Judge scores no points. At Banff he declined to assign himself any, because he must be at the door to admit the guests and saw no sense in exposing his flaws.

Section 4. The Judge's rulings are final. Appeals are heard never.

Section 5. The Judge is an optimist who believes everyone is good and that people sometimes make bad decisions. He prefers a culture of positivity to a punitive one. Negative rulings remain available, and fines do not.

## Article III. The Contestants

Section 1. The contestants are Jen, Kathy, and Blas. The list is fixed for the length of the competition.

Section 2. No advantage from an earlier contest carries over. Jen in particular holds no advantage from having recently been fired, because she has not recently been fired.

Section 3. Bo is not traveling. Bo earned one point for the salmon and one for the halibut he sent the Judge for Father's Day, and the two points went to John and Kathy, one each.

## Article IV. Rulings and points

Section 1. Excellence earns points. The Banff examples are helping the Judge put his bag in the overhead bin, saving him the last piece of dark chocolate, carrying his towel to the hot tub, finding warm pain au chocolat, and securing the corner table in a restaurant.

Section 2. Lapses lose points. The Judge has taken points for rolling eyes at an announcement and for planning to game or protest the system before it starts. A negative ruling that proves false may be rescinded.

Section 3. The same conduct may draw a ruling in each direction. At Banff, Jen was awarded +1 and -1 for talking a lot.

Section 4. Bribery is frowned upon. Flattery is not. At Banff, scoring opened at O'Hare security unless dark chocolate reached the Judge's doorstep first.

Section 5. Nobody edits the log. A mistaken ruling is undone by an offsetting ruling with the reason "correction," and the Judge tells the Clerk.

Section 6. Contestants rank by total points. A tie goes to the contestant with the most points earned (the sum of positive rulings), then to the one with the fewest points lost (the sum of negative rulings). A tie on all three shares the rank.

Section 7. First place is platinum, second is zinc, and third is wood.

Section 8. All scoring runs on UTC, and the Sheet stamps each ruling itself, so the Judge never enters a date or time. Days follow the party's clock: Chicago midnight until departure, Auckland midnight after arrival. The International Date Line removes Oct. 29, and the competition has 22 scoring days.

## Article V. The standings

Section 1. The standings page is at mrg0114.github.io/contest-standings/ and needs no account or app. It shows the race, each contestant's last 4 and 24 hours, the daily awards, the odds board, and every ruling with the Judge's reason.

Section 2. Anyone who turns on notifications receives the day's standings at 7 PM party time (Chicago time until departure, Auckland time after), an alert whenever someone takes the lead, the notice that the standings have gone dark, and the final results.

Section 3. The page names a holder for each award or leaves it vacant. The Platinum, Zinc, and Wood medals follow the tiers. Hot streak goes to the best net change in the last 24 hours, and Cold snap to the worst. Comeback kid goes to the contestant who gained the most places since yesterday. Teacher's pet goes to the most positive rulings in 24 hours, and Villain to the most negative. Judge's attention goes to the contestant with the most rulings overall.

Section 4. The odds board is for entertainment only, and contestants are asked to gamble irresponsibly. The house always wins, and the house is the Judge.

Section 5. The standings go dark for the final 24 hours, from 6:20 PM CST on Wednesday, Nov. 11 (1:20 PM NZDT on Thursday, Nov. 12). The page shows a countdown, and rulings continue. The final results appear when the aircraft leaves Auckland: 1:20 PM NZDT on Friday, Nov. 13 (6:20 PM CST on Thursday, Nov. 12).

## Article VI. The Platinum seat

Section 1. One guest enters the Platinum Dining Area with the Judge. The contestant who finishes in platinum holds the claim to the seat.

Section 2. The zinc and wood finishers take their titles and the main lounge. The main lounge has a buffet with slow-cooked venison, a gelato fridge, New Zealand wine and beer, and a flat white the reviews praise. Point Hacks calls it a pleasant place to relax. The Judge recommends the passionfruit cooler.

Section 3. The party reaches the lounge before the aircraft leaves, and the final results appear at takeoff. The Sheet stays open to the Judge throughout the final 24 hours. He names the guest from the standings as the Sheet shows them when he reaches the door.

Section 4. If two contestants share platinum under Article IV, Section 6, the Judge chooses between them, and his choice is final.

## Article VII. Travel provisions

Section 1. Each traveler needs a New Zealand tourist visa. It is applied for online by logging into the American Airlines account and opening My Trips, where each traveler also updates passport and emergency contact information. The Judge holds dinner planning to be equally important.

Section 2. The flight from Dallas/Fort Worth to Auckland leaves at 11:55 PM CDT. Dinner begins about 45 minutes after takeoff, and by the time the main course is cleared it will feel like 1:30 AM. Breakfast is served two hours before landing, the galley keeps sandwiches, fruit, and snacks out all flight, and most flights add an optional beef tenderloin slider between services.

Section 3. Three dining strategies are lawful.

a) Maximize sleep. Dine beforehand at the Flagship Lounge in Terminal D at Dallas/Fort Worth, tell the flight attendant dinner is skipped, change into pajamas, and sleep.

b) Express dining. Ask the flight attendant for Express Dining after boarding. The starter, main course, and dessert arrive together on one tray after takeoff.

c) The full midnight dinner. Warm nuts and a premium drink, an appetizer, a side salad, a pre-ordered main of beef filet, sea bass, or pasta, and the signature ice cream sundae.

The Judge is not traveling 8,290 miles to skip second dinner.

Section 4. The long layover in Dallas/Fort Worth is intentional. It is a buffer against a delayed or canceled flight from O'Hare.

## Article VIII. The Clerk

Section 1. Matt Gallagher is the Clerk of the Standings. He keeps the Form, the Sheet, and the standings page, and he enters no rulings.

Section 2. The Clerk may change how the page looks and never what the Sheet holds. Page changes do not touch the data.

Section 3. The Judge may have a Paddock post taken down, and the Clerk hides it.

## Article IX. Amendment and the freeze

Section 1. While the competition runs, nobody changes the Form's questions or choices, the scoring rules, the start and end times, the list of contestants, or the rows in the Sheet.

Section 2. Display additions, prizes among them, are allowed.

Section 3. The Judge adopts amendments, and they are recorded below.

## Article X. Ratification

This Constitution takes effect on the Judge's signature and governs every ruling since the first one, at 3:26 PM CDT on Thursday, Oct. 22, 2026. The contestants may sign below, and their signatures earn no points.

a) The fan passcode posts under any name except the Judge's and the contestants'.

c) The Judge's passcode posts as Paul with a Judge badge, and his latest post stays pinned at the top.

Posts go up at once, run to 500 characters, and are limited to one every 10 seconds per name. Posts send no notifications. Keep it friendly-ish.
